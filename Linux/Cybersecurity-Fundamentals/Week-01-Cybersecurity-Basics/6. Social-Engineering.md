# Social Engineering

Social engineering is a type of attack that manipulates people into performing an action or revealing information.

Instead of relying only on technical vulnerabilities, social engineering targets human behavior.

## Common Social Engineering Techniques

### 1. Phishing

Phishing uses fraudulent messages designed to trick users into revealing information, clicking malicious links, or performing an unwanted action.

Common delivery methods include:

- Email
- Text messages
- Social media
- Fake websites

---

### 2. Pretexting

Pretexting involves creating a believable story or situation to convince a victim to provide information or perform an action.

---

### 3. Baiting

Baiting uses something attractive or interesting to encourage a victim to take an unsafe action.

For example, an attacker may use a fake file, download, or other tempting offer.

---

### 4. Tailgating

Tailgating is a physical social engineering technique where an unauthorized person attempts to enter a restricted area by following an authorized person.

---

### 5. Quid Pro Quo

Quid pro quo attacks offer something in exchange for information or an action.

For example, an attacker may pretend to provide technical support in exchange for login information.

---

## Social Engineering Attack Flow

A simplified social engineering attack can involve:

**Target Selection → Information Gathering → Creating Trust → Manipulation → Victim Action → Possible Compromise**

---

## Social Engineering in Security Labs

In cybersecurity labs, social engineering concepts can be studied in isolated and authorized environments.

The purpose is to understand:

- How phishing attacks work
- How fake websites can deceive users
- How attackers manipulate trust
- How users can recognize suspicious activity
- How organizations can defend against these attacks

All testing should be performed only on systems and accounts where explicit authorization has been given.

---

## How to Defend Against Social Engineering

- Verify unexpected requests
- Avoid clicking suspicious links
- Check website addresses carefully
- Use multi-factor authentication
- Never share passwords or OTPs
- Be cautious with unexpected attachments
- Report suspicious messages
- Provide security awareness training
