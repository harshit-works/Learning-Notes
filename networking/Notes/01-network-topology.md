# Network Topology

## What is Network Topology?

Network topology is the physical or logical arrangement of devices in a network. It defines how computers, routers, switches, and other devices are connected and how data flows between them.

## Types of Network Topology

### 1. Bus Topology
- All devices are connected to a single backbone cable.
- Easy to install.
- Failure of the backbone cable affects the entire network.

### 2. Star Topology
- All devices connect to a central switch or hub.
- Easy to troubleshoot.
- Failure of one cable affects only one device.
- Failure of the central switch affects the whole network.

### 3. Ring Topology
- Devices are connected in a circular path.
- Data travels from one device to the next.
- Failure of one connection can interrupt communication.

### 4. Mesh Topology
- Every device is connected to every other device.
- Provides high reliability.
- Expensive due to many connections.

### 5. Tree Topology
- Combination of multiple star networks.
- Common in large organizations.

### 6. Hybrid Topology
- Combination of two or more topologies.
- Flexible and scalable.



## Real-world Examples

- Home Network → Star Topology
- Office Network → Star Topology
- Internet → Mesh Topology
